Roundtable · Post-AGI governance · Sentient Futures, Post-Control Initiative · 20 October 2026

What makes cooperation without control work?

A 90-minute virtual roundtable on a post-AGI question: what would make cooperation between humans and advanced AI systems possible and trustworthy if humans can no longer reliably control them? We held that premise fixed instead of debating whether or when it happens, and looked at credibility from both sides: why humans might trust an AI, and why an AI might trust humans to keep their side.

I designed and hosted it, with Alejandra Leyva co-hosting, under the Post-Control Initiative mentored by Julia Bossmann. About ten participants took part, working in AI governance and policy, technical safety research, strategic foresight and field building, from several countries.

The session moved in five steps: the main obstacles to cooperation; concrete arrangements that would give both sides a reason to keep cooperating; how each arrangement could fail, from either side; what evidence would show each side is keeping its commitments, and whether that evidence could mislead; and which claims researchers could test with current models, simulations or formal models. Alongside the script I built a capture matrix that records, for each proposed mechanism, why humans and AI would each cooperate, its critical assumption, its failure mode and a possible test. Threads that came up included who gets to speak for humans in any agreement, transparency as evidence of compliance, and program equilibrium as a formal model of credible commitment.

It ran under the Chatham House Rule, with consented, de-identified transcription. An unattributed synthesis for participants is in preparation.
