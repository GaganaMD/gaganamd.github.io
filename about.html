About

How I got here.

I study whether the evidence we collect about AI agents is good enough to act on.

In practice, that means three questions. Does a monitor see a problem early enough for anyone to stop it? Does a benchmark score reflect what the agent actually did? And does an environment a model wrote encode the situation we asked for? I work on them with small, controlled experiments: the protocol is frozen before the results come in, the code is tested, and every write-up says what the result doesn't show.

I got here through applied ML. My first projects were satellite imagery, hyperspectral unmixing (a wavelet framework with its own wavelet-shaped activation function, now an IEEE paper) and humanitarian forecasting, mostly in teams through Omdena and Neuromatch. At IISc's DREAM Lab I worked on co-scheduling drone routes and analytics across edge and fog computing, which became a co-authored paper. Building a benchmark for long-horizon finance agents at BaseThesis Labs then showed me how easily an evaluation can look rigorous and still measure the wrong thing.

Right now I'm a research fellow at PRISM, Sentient Futures and FlowResearch. Most of my results come from one environment or one small model. The next thing I want to learn is how to test these ideas at scale, ideally in a funded fellowship, a research assistant post, or a PhD.

Outside research, I help run mentoring and technical workshops for women in technology.
